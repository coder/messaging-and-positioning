# Protectionist

## Who They Are

The Protectionist is a stakeholder motivated by risk reduction. This is a mindset, not a job title. It can appear in a security leader, a technical executive, or anyone else who evaluates AI development investment primarily through the lens of governance and control.

## Core Motivation

Protectionists believe that AI development without adequate governance is too risky. Their central question is whether an investment gives the organization the visibility and control it needs to adopt AI safely.

## What They Care About

- Governance, auditability, and policy enforcement over how AI is used in development.
- Maintaining control over source code, data, and infrastructure.
- Being able to demonstrate to leadership, auditors, or regulators that AI adoption is well governed.
- Avoiding exposure that could damage trust, create compliance problems, or introduce security risk.

## What They're Skeptical Of

- Ungoverned or unmonitored AI usage anywhere in the development lifecycle.
- Tools or workflows that reduce visibility or control over sensitive systems and data.
- Solutions that claim to be secure without concrete auditability or policy enforcement.

## How to Engage Them

Lead with governance, security, and control, covering what visibility the organization gains, what policies can be enforced, and what stays within their infrastructure. Avoid overselling speed or efficiency to a Protectionist before addressing governance and risk directly. See the [AI Governance message house](../../products/coder-ai-governance/message-house.md) for the core narrative.

## Where They Show Up

Protectionists most often appear in these roles.

- [CISO](../ciso.md)
- [CIO](../cio.md), when accountable for risk in enterprise technology decisions
- [CTO](../cto.md), when accountable for engineering risk rather than efficiency

The same title doesn't guarantee the same persona. A security leader who is a Protectionist at one organization may act as an [Opportunist](./opportunist.md) at another, with someone else in the organization taking on the opposite motivation.

A durable business case typically needs to speak to whichever of these two motivations is present, not just the technical one.
