# Buyer Personas

The pages in this directory describe three motivational personas Coder encounters when selling AI development infrastructure, [Caring Provider](./caring-provider.md), [Opportunist](./opportunist.md), and [Protectionist](./protectionist.md).

## How this differs from the other `audiences/` pages

The role-based pages elsewhere in `audiences/` (developer, platform engineer, CISO, CTO, and so on) describe a job function, including its responsibilities, workflows, and success metrics. The personas in this directory describe something different, namely the underlying motivation a stakeholder brings to an AI development or infrastructure decision, independent of their title.

These personas are not job titles, and they don't map one-to-one to roles. The same title can house a different persona at different organizations, and a single stakeholder can hold more than one persona depending on the situation. For example, a CISO at one organization might act as a Protectionist (worried that ungoverned AI development is too risky), while at another organization a CISO might act as an Opportunist (worried that AI development is too costly or inefficient), with a CIO or another leader taking on the Protectionist role instead. Job titles are a starting point for identifying a persona, not a substitute for it.

## The three personas

- **Caring Provider** — a technical practitioner motivated by delivering a great experience to the people who depend on them. Caring providers are usually the entry point for adoption, but they don't always hold budget.
- **Opportunist** — a stakeholder motivated by cost and efficiency, who evaluates AI development investment through the lens of return on investment and avoiding wasted spend.
- **Protectionist** — a stakeholder motivated by risk reduction, who evaluates AI development investment through the lens of governance, security, and control.

Caring providers most often generate initial interest and technical validation. Opportunists and protectionists most often hold the budget and final decision authority, and a durable business case typically needs to address both motivations, not just the technical one.

## Using these pages

When writing or reviewing messaging aimed at a specific stakeholder, first identify which persona is most relevant to what they care about, then combine that persona's guidance with the relevant role-based `audiences/` page and the applicable `products/` message house. A persona page describes *why* someone is engaging; a role-based audience page describes *what* they do day to day.
