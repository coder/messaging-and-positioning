## One-Line Positioning

Domino governs the AI a company ships, meaning the models, AI apps, and agents it builds and deploys on Domino, while Coder governs how all of its software gets built, by developers and AI agents, on any infrastructure.

## Strategic Themes and Patterns

- **Relationship.** Competitive, with partial overlap. Domino governs what ships, meaning the models, AI apps, and agents that pass through its approvals and deployment gates, with monitoring after release and an audit trail throughout. Coder governs where software gets built, for every developer and coding agent. Both provide governed workspaces with coding agents, which is the infrastructure interactive development runs on. Domino Workspaces run as [Kubernetes pods](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture) with [persistent volumes that are kept across stop and start](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/infrastructure-and-compute/manage-compute-resources/manage-persistent-volumes), serving [Jupyter, RStudio, VS Code, and other environments](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/index), and Coder [provisions workspaces through Terraform](https://coder.com/docs/tutorials/template-from-scratch) on infrastructure the customer controls. The overlap stops at that workspace layer. Domino's [model registry](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/models/register-and-govern/model-registry), [model endpoints](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/model-deployment/index), [app and agent hosting](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/agents/index), and [model governance](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/index) are outside Coder's scope.
- **Positioning shift.** Domino's [homepage](https://domino.ai/) now leads with "The platform to build, scale, and govern AI-powered applications", and its [platform page](https://domino.ai/platform) calls Domino "The operating system for AI business outcomes". The homepage description still mentions "code-first data science teams", and the 6.3 docs still name [data scientists, quantitative researchers, and AI/ML engineers](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/who-domino-is-for) as core users. Calling Domino a data science tool understates how it now positions itself.
- **Shared component.** Domino serves browser-based VS Code ["with the power of code-server from Coder.com"](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace/launch-a-vs-code-workspace). [code-server](https://github.com/coder/code-server) is Coder's open-source project. Using it inside Domino does not make a Domino workspace a Coder deployment, and Coder is not listed on Domino's [partners page](https://domino.ai/partners).
- **Choice, Control, Consistency.** Control is not the main tradeoff, since Domino also supports [customer-managed, on-premises, and air-gapped deployments](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/deployment-options). Consistency is not the main tradeoff either, since Domino tracks code, data, software, commands, and results through its [Reproducibility Engine](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/reproducibility/index). Git providers are not the difference, since Domino's Git-based projects support [GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, and other providers such as Azure DevOps](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/projects/create-projects). The tradeoff is Choice of workload shape and tooling. Domino workspaces are Linux containers on Kubernetes, with [Ubuntu-based standard environments](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/manage-compute-environments/8-compute-environment-catalog) and validation on [EKS, AKS, GKE, and Red Hat OpenShift](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/kubernetes-infrastructure). The selected environment [determines the available IDEs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace), and Domino documents [custom environments](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/custom-environments) as the way to standardize coding assistants beyond Copilot, Claude Code, and Codex. Coder templates can target [VMs, containers, Kubernetes, and Windows](../products/coder-workspaces/message-house.md) on any infrastructure Terraform can manage, with any IDE or agent.
- **Lock-in risk.** The risk is scope, not data. An organization that extends an AI platform to cover general software engineering ties every developer's environment to that platform's environment model, supported IDEs, and release cycle. Domino says it ships [major or minor releases approximately every 3 months](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/kubernetes-infrastructure), while self-managed 6.0.0, 6.1.0, and 6.2.0 shipped in [November 2024, June 2025, and January 2026](https://docs.dominodatalab.com/release_notes/6-2-3/) and 6.3.0 in [September 2026](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/installation/releases), and self-managed tiers include [up to 2 upgrades per year](https://domino.ai/pricing). Each release validates specific Kubernetes versions, which for 6.3.0 are EKS, AKS, and GKE 1.34 and 1.35 and OpenShift 4.21 ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/kubernetes-infrastructure)). In Coder's view, moving developer environments off that platform later means rebuilding them elsewhere rather than reconfiguring them.
- **Build versus buy.** For organizations that want one governed environment layer for software engineers and data scientists, the realistic alternative to Coder is usually an internal platform built on Kubernetes, VMs, and JupyterHub or code-server. See [Why Coder](../company/why-coder.md).
- **Where Domino is sufficient on its own.** Data science, quantitative research, and statistical programming teams that need governed model development, deployment, and model risk management in one product, and that don't need to standardize software engineering environments on the same platform.

## Coder's Strengths Here

- **Any software, not just AI artifacts.** Coder governs the environments for any kind of software, from web apps and backend services to ML work. Coder's primary users are developers and coding agents, with platform teams managing the platform ([Coder Message House](../company/message-house.md)), and it also serves [data scientists and ML engineers](../use-cases/ml-operations.md). Domino's docs describe builders ["working with code to build models, apps, analyses, or agents"](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/who-domino-is-for), mainly data scientists, quantitative researchers, and AI/ML engineers, plus biostatisticians, statistical programmers, analysts, and actuaries. Domino's [overview](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/domino-overview) also positions it as a governed app platform for "non-expert coders" building internal tools, and its [coding assistants page](https://domino.ai/platform/coding-assistants) is titled "AI Coding Assistants for Data Science". We found no Domino docs or marketing aimed at software engineering teams.
- **Desktop IDE access built into the workspace agent.** Coder's [workspace agent](https://coder.com/docs/reference/glossary) itself provides SSH, port forwarding, and IDE connections, so VS Code, Cursor, JetBrains IDEs, Zed, and other desktop IDEs connect through it ([Coder Docs](https://coder.com/docs/user-guides/workspace-access)). In self-managed Domino 6.3, workspace SSH is [off by default](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/configuration/central-configuration) until an administrator enables it, and users need an environment with `openssh-server` and the [Dom CLI](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace/streamline-local-development-ssh). Domino says its [base images include `openssh-server`](https://domino.ai/resources/blueprints/getting-started-domino-vs-code-extension). Adding a browser IDE beyond the defaults takes admin work on both platforms: Coder template admins add them with [web IDE modules](https://coder.com/docs/admin/templates/extending-templates/web-ides), and in Domino ["an administrator or advanced user"](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/work-with-pluggable-workspaces/add-workspace-ides) typically edits the environment's Dockerfile and workspace tool definition.
- **Workload shapes beyond Linux pods.** Coder templates provision VMs, containers, Kubernetes pods, and [Windows workspaces](https://coder.com/docs/user-guides/workspace-access/remote-desktops) ([Coder Workspaces message house](../products/coder-workspaces/message-house.md)). Domino workspaces run as [Kubernetes pods](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture) on Linux images: the [standard images are Ubuntu-based](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/manage-compute-environments/8-compute-environment-catalog), and the [custom image requirements](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/use-external-images/automatic-custom-image-compatibility) are met by Linux distributions. GPUs are not the difference, since Domino documents NVIDIA GPUs, including [Multi-Instance GPU partitions](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace), and [AWS Trainium and Inferentia accelerators](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/use-external-images/aws-accelerators).
- **Built-in, generally available agent governance.** [AI Governance](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/ai-governance), included in Premium and generally available since Coder v2.30, includes AI Gateway, which audits AI sessions and enforces policy and [budgets](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/ai-gateway/cost-controls) for [supported clients](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/ai-gateway/clients) such as Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, and GitHub Copilot. Cursor is not a supported AI Gateway client today ([Coder Docs](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/ai-gateway/clients)). Domino documents routing coding agents through [LLM Gateway 2.0](https://docs.domino.ai/cloud/platform-capabilities/features/llms/llm-gateway), which is in [early access](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/reuse-credentials) and deployed as a separate app on request.
- **Model credentials stay out of the workspace.** [Coder Agents](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/agents) runs its agent loop in the Coder control plane, so LLM provider credentials never enter the workspace. By default, Domino's coding assistants sign in from inside the workspace with each user's own [Anthropic, GitHub, or OpenAI account](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/get-started), and Domino documents reusing long-lived tokens by storing them as [encrypted user environment variables that it injects into every run](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/reuse-credentials).
- **Free, open-source starting point.** Coder Community is free and open source, with unlimited self-hosted workspaces ([Packaging](../company/packaging.md), [coder.com/pricing](https://coder.com/pricing)). Domino [prices by quote](https://domino.ai/pricing), and we found no Domino free tier or free trial.
- **Scheduled start and stop, with persistence the template defines.** When template admins enable them, Coder workspaces can [start automatically in the morning and stop after the user's absence is detected](https://coder.com/docs/user-guides/workspace-scheduling), and [templates control](https://coder.com/docs/admin/templates/extending-templates/resource-persistence) which resources persist. Domino also keeps durable workspace volumes across stop and start, but by default only files under `/mnt` persist, and files outside it, including installed packages, are lost unless an administrator enables [package and home directory persistence](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/use-package-persistence/index) ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/manage-workspaces)). Domino's documented auto-shutdown, which is [off by default](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/configuration/central-configuration), measures [time since the workspace started](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/set-up-a-workspace), not idle time, and we found no documented scheduled autostart. One Domino audit event describes sessions stopping ["due to inactivity"](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/audit-trail/audit-trail-glossary), so an idle stop may exist that the docs don't describe.

## Domino Data Lab Overview

- **What it is.** An enterprise AI platform for ["building, delivering, and governing AI solutions for mission-critical use cases"](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/domino-overview), organized as an AI Factory, an App & Agent Hub, and a Governance Center on top of multi-cloud and hybrid compute orchestration. Domino lists five common uses: a governed app platform for "non-expert coders", agent execution orchestration, model development and MLOps, model risk management in financial services, and a statistical computing environment for pharmaceutical biostatistics ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/domino-overview)).
- **Company.** Domino Data Lab, Inc. was [founded in 2013](https://domino.ai/company) and is venture-backed, with investors including Sequoia Capital, Coatue Management, NVIDIA, NetApp, Snowflake, and UBS. Thomas Robinson, previously COO, became [CEO in August 2026](https://domino.ai/press-releases/thomas-robinson), and co-founder Nick Elprin is now Chief Product Officer, President, and chairman.
- **Architecture.** A set of [containerized, Kubernetes-native applications](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture). Workspaces, jobs, and model endpoints run as pods, and Compute Environments are [based on Docker containers](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture), with selectable [hardware tiers, including GPUs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace). Workspaces can attach [Spark, Ray, Dask, and MPI clusters](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace), and Domino 6.3 adds [Slurm on Kubernetes](https://docs.domino.ai/release-notes/6-3-0), which administrators enable when they deploy a Data Plane. [Nexus](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture/nexus-hybrid-architecture) runs workloads across multiple Kubernetes clusters, regions, clouds, or on-premises sites.
- **Workspaces and IDEs.** The Domino Standard Environment includes [VS Code, JupyterLab, RStudio, and Jupyter](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/get-started), with VS Code [served by code-server](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace/launch-a-vs-code-workspace). Licensed [SAS](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/work-with-pluggable-workspaces/sas-workspace), [MATLAB](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/use-external-images/matlab), and [Stata](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/use-external-images/stata) run from custom images, and [Posit Workbench](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/use-external-images/posit-workbench) adds the Positron IDE. Desktop VS Code, Cursor, PyCharm, and ParaView connect [over SSH](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace/streamline-local-development-ssh), and Domino has verified [Positron over SSH](https://docs.dominodatalab.com/release_notes/6-1-1/). Domino also publishes a [VS Code extension](https://domino.ai/resources/blueprints/getting-started-domino-vs-code-extension) that manages projects, jobs, and workspaces and opens the SSH connection.
- **AI features.** Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, and Codex are [pre-installed in the Domino Standard Environment](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/index). All three run as VS Code extensions, and Claude Code and Codex also run as CLI tools in other workspace IDEs ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/get-started)). Domino Cloud's [release notes](https://docs.domino.ai/release-notes/cloud-release) list them in March 2026, and self-managed [Domino 6.3](https://docs.domino.ai/release-notes/6-3-0) includes them, though Domino's [May 2026 announcement](https://domino.ai/press-releases/app-hub) described them as private preview with general availability by Q3 2026. Users sign in with their own [Anthropic, GitHub, or OpenAI accounts](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/get-started), and Claude Code can also authenticate through Vertex or Bedrock. [Domino Skills](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/reference/skills) add platform actions such as submitting Jobs and load in Claude Code, Codex, and GitHub Copilot, and only Claude Code also gets the bundled MCP server. Separately from the coding assistants, Domino [traces, evaluates, and hosts agentic systems](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/agents/index) that users build, and [hosts LLMs behind an OpenAI-compatible API](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/llms/host-an-llm).
- **Governance.** Governance bundles connect project work to policies that define the [evidence, approvals, and gates](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/index) required for models, apps, agents, endpoints, and files, and policies can follow frameworks such as SR 11-7 or NIST AI RMF. The supported gate action is [Deploy](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/gating-domino-governance). An [audit trail](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/audit-trail/index) records user actions and, when enabled, [file access to Domino Datasets and NetApp Volumes inside workspaces](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/audit-trail/workspace-file-access-events/index), and retains data for up to 30 years depending on the deployment's retention policy. Domino lists Governance as an [add-on](https://domino.ai/pricing).
- **Target customer.** Regulated enterprises in [life sciences, financial services and insurance, and the public sector](https://domino.ai/press-releases/gartner-mq-visionary-2026). Its [company page](https://domino.ai/company) names customers such as GSK, AstraZeneca, Novartis, UBS, Allstate, and Fannie Mae.
- **Deployment model.** Domino Cloud is [single-tenant SaaS](https://domino.ai/pricing) that runs workloads in [AWS and Azure regions](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/deployment-options). Customer-managed deployments run on AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, or Oracle Cloud Infrastructure ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/deployment-options)), with validation on [EKS, AKS, GKE, and Red Hat OpenShift](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/kubernetes-infrastructure). We found no OCI install guide or OCI entry in the validation matrix. On-premises deployments most often use [Red Hat OpenShift](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture/domino-on-eks-aks-gke), hybrid deployments use Nexus, and Domino documents [offline (air-gapped) installation](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/installation/manage-deployments/private-offline). Domino's docs say it can run in [IL6 and IL7 air-gapped environments](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/deployment-options), and its [platform page](https://domino.ai/platform) says it is deployed in DoD IL5 environments. The current self-managed release is [6.3.0 (September 2026)](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/installation/releases), and Domino Cloud ships separately, most recently on [September 30, 2026](https://docs.domino.ai/release-notes/cloud-release).

## Coder Overview for This Comparison

- **Self-hosted workspaces defined as code.** The Coder server essentially runs a Terraform apply every time a workspace is created, started, or stopped ([Coder Docs](https://coder.com/docs/tutorials/template-from-scratch)), so platform teams define environments on any infrastructure Terraform can manage, including [air-gapped deployments with all features](https://coder.com/docs/install/prepare/airgap).
- **Data science and ML workloads.** GPU and CPU workspaces next to governed training data, with IDEs such as VS Code or PyCharm ([ML Operations use case](../use-cases/ml-operations.md)) and browser-based [JupyterLab and RStudio](https://coder.com/docs/user-guides/workspace-access).
- **Governed AI agents.** [AI Governance](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/ai-governance) (including AI Gateway) is included in Premium. [Coder Agents](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/agents) runs its agent loop in the Coder control plane, executes work in workspaces, and routes all of its model traffic through AI Gateway ([Coder AI Governance message house](../products/coder-ai-governance/message-house.md)).
- **Any Git provider.** GitHub, GitLab, Bitbucket, and Azure DevOps through [external authentication](https://coder.com/docs/admin/external-auth). Domino's [Git-based projects](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/projects/create-projects) support GitHub, GitLab, and Bitbucket by name, and Azure DevOps through app username and password authentication.

## Where Domino Data Lab Is Strong or Coder Has a Gap

- **Model and AI application lifecycle.** Domino includes an MLflow-based [experiment manager](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/development/track-monitor) and [model registry](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/models/register-and-govern/model-registry), synchronous and asynchronous [model endpoints](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/model-deployment/index), and batch scoring and [export to Amazon SageMaker, NVIDIA Fleet Command, Snowflake, and CI/CD pipelines](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/models/index). It also provides [monitoring for data drift and model quality](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/monitoring/index), [Flows](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/flows/index) for pipelines built on Flyte, [apps](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/apps/apps-in-domino/index) built with Streamlit, Dash, Shiny, or Flask, self-serve [Launchers](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/launchers/index), and [agent hosting](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/agents/index). Coder does not provide a model registry, model serving, or hosting for published apps. Coder's [shared ports](https://coder.com/docs/admin/networking/port-forwarding) expose services running in a workspace during development.
- **Model governance for regulated industries.** Domino's governance features enforce [approvals and deployment gates](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/index) on models, apps, and agents. Domino states that it [supports customer compliance with frameworks such as 21 CFR Part 11, EudraLex, WHO Annex 5, and GxP](https://domino.ai/security), documents an optional [e-signature and reason-for-change workflow](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/operations/audit-logs/esignature), and lists ISO 27001, ISO 9001, and SOC 2 Type 2 on its [Trust Center](https://trust.domino.ai). Coder AI Governance governs agent and model access in development environments. It does not do model risk management.
- **Governing what ships, in practice.** Policies define the stages, evidence, and approvals a bundle must pass, and approvals can expire and require [periodic revalidation](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/approvals). [Gates](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/gating-domino-governance) block deploying a published app, agent, or model endpoint until the required approvals are complete, can apply to specific hardware tiers or Data Planes, and record each decision for audit. Typical situations these features target, not named customers: a bank holding a credit-risk model until model risk signs off under [SR 11-7](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/index), a pharmaceutical team reproducing a clinical analysis for a regulator with the [Reproducibility Engine](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/reproducibility/index) under [21 CFR Part 11](https://domino.ai/security), and a production model watched for [data drift and quality](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/monitoring/index) after release. Gates cover deployments through Domino only, preview deployments of app drafts are not gated, and the [model registry](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/models/register-and-govern/model-registry) can deploy to externally hosted endpoints, for which we found no documented gate. Governance is an [add-on](https://domino.ai/pricing), and we could not confirm which of these features require it.
- **Experiment reproducibility.** The [Domino Reproducibility Engine](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/reproducibility/index) records code, data, software, commands, and results at select operations such as running Jobs, syncing workspaces, or publishing endpoints, and it tracks data stored inside Domino. Coder reproduces environments through templates but does not track experiment inputs and outputs.
- **Data science compute and data access.** Domino workspaces attach [Spark, Ray, Dask, and MPI clusters](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace), mount [Datasets, NetApp Volumes, and External Data Volumes](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/data/index), and run [Slurm](https://docs.domino.ai/release-notes/6-3-0) workloads in 6.3. In Coder, the platform team builds equivalent access into templates.
- **File access audit inside workspaces.** When enabled, Domino records reads, writes, and other file operations on Datasets and NetApp Volumes inside workspaces [at the kernel level, regardless of access method](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/audit-trail/workspace-file-access-events/index). Coder's [workspace process logging](https://coder.com/docs/admin/templates/extending-templates/process-logging) records the processes started in a workspace, and its [audit logs](https://coder.com/docs/admin/security/audit-logs) record platform actions, but Coder does not document file-level access auditing.
- **Pre-installed coding assistants with platform skills.** Domino ships Claude Code, Copilot, and Codex in its standard environment, with [Domino Skills](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/reference/skills), which all three load, and a bundled MCP server, in Claude Code only, for platform actions such as running Jobs. In Coder, platform teams add third-party agents to templates, or use [Coder Agents](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/agents), which needs no agent software in the workspace.
- **Prompt and response guardrails, in early access.** Domino's [LLM Gateway 2.0](https://docs.domino.ai/cloud/platform-capabilities/features/llms/llm-gateway), [in early access](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/reuse-credentials), lists usage tracking, per-team and per-model budgets, and guardrails for prompt and response inspection. Coder's [AI Gateway](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/ai-gateway) documents session auditing and cost controls, and we found no documented prompt or response guardrails.
- **Recognition among data science platforms.** Domino reports being named a Visionary in the [2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for AI Platforms for Data Science and Machine Learning](https://domino.ai/press-releases/gartner-mq-visionary-2026), its third consecutive year counting two placements under the report's earlier title (company announcement).

## Known Limitations in Domino Data Lab's Approach

- **Usable for general software, not built for an engineering org (as of 2026-10-13).** Developers can write general software in Domino workspaces, which include VS Code and [Git-based projects](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/projects/git-based-projects), and Domino hosts apps built with [React and FastAPI](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture). Every workspace lives inside a Domino [project](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/projects/create-projects) organized around data, experiments, and models, and the limitations below make Domino a poor fit as the environment layer for a whole software engineering organization.
- **Linux-only workspace images (as of 2026-10-13).** Domino's standard environments are [Ubuntu-based](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/manage-compute-environments/8-compute-environment-catalog), and its [custom image requirements](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/use-external-images/automatic-custom-image-compatibility) (bash, glibc, and shadow-utils) are met by Linux distributions such as Ubuntu, Debian, RHEL, and CentOS. We found no documented Windows workspace support. External Data Volumes can [mount Windows SMB shares](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/data/external-volumes/index), which is a storage feature.
- **Browser IDEs tied to the environment (as of 2026-10-13).** The selected Compute Environment [determines the available IDEs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace), and adding a browser IDE requires Dockerfile and workspace tool configuration changes ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/work-with-pluggable-workspaces/add-workspace-ides)).
- **Desktop IDE access requires setup (as of 2026-10-13).** SSH access needs an SSH-enabled environment, which Domino says its base images are ([Domino blueprint](https://domino.ai/resources/blueprints/getting-started-domino-vs-code-extension)), and the Dom CLI on the user's machine ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace/streamline-local-development-ssh)). In self-managed 6.3, SSH is [off by default](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/configuration/central-configuration) until an administrator enables it, and Domino Cloud users [contact Domino Support](https://docs.domino.ai/cloud/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace/streamline-local-development-ssh) if the option isn't available. Domino's [platform page](https://domino.ai/platform) markets "one-click access to IDEs like Cursor, Jupyter, RStudio, and VS Code", and its [AI Workbench page](https://domino.ai/platform/ai-workbench) and docs describe desktop IDE access over SSH. We found no documented method other than SSH for attaching a desktop IDE.
- **Persistence scoped to `/mnt` by default (as of 2026-10-13).** Domino's architecture docs describe workspaces as [ephemeral pods that must be synced to long-term storage](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture), and each durable workspace keeps a [persistent volume](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/infrastructure-and-compute/manage-compute-resources/manage-persistent-volumes) across stop and start. Only files under `/mnt` persist by default ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/manage-workspaces)). By default, Domino limits each user to four workspaces per project and 16 across projects, and administrators can change both limits ([Domino docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/configuration/central-configuration)).
- **No Git LFS (as of 2026-10-13).** Domino [does not support Git Large File Storage](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/projects/git-based-projects), and workspace setup can fail for repositories with [files larger than 2 GB](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/projects/create-projects).
- **No secure environment-level secrets for coding agents (as of 2026-10-13).** Domino [does not currently provide a secure mechanism to inject secrets](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/custom-environments) through environment variables at the Compute Environment level. It documents per-user sign-in, [encrypted user environment variables](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/reuse-credentials), and [project environment variables](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/manage-compute-environments/manage-environment-variables/2-build-project-variables) that "can hold sensitive information, such as credentials" instead, with LLM Gateway 2.0, in early access, as the documented way to route agent traffic through centrally governed model access.
- **LLM gateway in transition (as of 2026-10-13).** Domino Cloud [removed the legacy AI Gateway](https://docs.domino.ai/release-notes/cloud-release) in its September 3, 2026 release, and it is [no longer enabled by default in 6.3](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/llms/ai-gateway). Its successor, LLM Gateway 2.0, is [in early access](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/reuse-credentials) and [distributed as a Domino-deployed app](https://docs.domino.ai/cloud/platform-capabilities/features/llms/llm-gateway) on request, with native integration planned.

## Common Questions

- **We already run Domino. Why would we add Coder?** Domino governs the models, AI apps, and agents a company builds and deploys on Domino, through [approvals and deployment gates](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/governance/index). Coder governs where all software gets built, and standardizes environments for software developers and their agents across [many IDEs](https://coder.com/docs/user-guides/workspace-access), [Linux, macOS, and Windows](https://coder.com/pricing), and any infrastructure Terraform can manage. Many organizations need both scopes, and Coder does not replace Domino's model lifecycle features.
- **Can Domino host our software engineering teams?** Developers can write code there, so don't claim Domino can't build software. It isn't built for a whole engineering org (see Known Limitations). Domino's docs describe builders of [models, apps, analyses, or agents](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/who-domino-is-for), mainly data scientists, quantitative researchers, and AI/ML engineers, and Domino hosts apps built with frameworks including [React and FastAPI](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/architecture-and-deployment/architecture). Its [overview](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/domino-overview) positions it as a governed app platform for "non-expert coders" building internal tools, and we found no Domino docs or marketing positioning it for software engineering teams. Its workspaces use [Ubuntu-based images](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/manage-compute-environments/8-compute-environment-catalog), and the [environment determines the available IDEs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace).
- **Domino's VS Code is code-server. Is that Coder?** It is Coder's open-source [code-server](https://github.com/coder/code-server), which Domino [credits in its docs](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/workspaces/launch-a-workspace/launch-a-vs-code-workspace). The Coder platform adds Terraform-defined workspaces on any infrastructure, IDEs beyond code-server, and AI Governance.
- **Domino has coding agents built in. Isn't that the same as Coder?** Both run AI coding agents in governed environments, at different levels of maturity and scope. Domino's agents work inside Domino projects for its AI and data science users, while Coder governs every developer's and agent's environment across teams. Domino pre-installs [Claude Code, Copilot, and Codex](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/index), documents [custom environments](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/custom-environments) for other agents, and documents routing agent traffic through [LLM Gateway 2.0](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/features/coding-assistants/reuse-credentials), which is in early access. Coder runs third-party agents in workspaces, where the template's [network policy](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/agents/platform-controls/template-optimization#configure-network-boundaries) applies to every agent and process, and [AI Governance](https://coder.com/docs/ai-coder/ai-governance) adds AI Gateway for supported clients.
- **Do both support air-gapped deployment?** Yes. Domino documents [offline (air-gapped) installation](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/admin/installation/manage-deployments/private-offline) and says it can run in [IL6 and IL7 environments](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-overview/deployment-options), and Coder supports [air-gapped deployments with all features](https://coder.com/docs/install/prepare/airgap).
- **Can we use Coder for ML engineers instead of Domino?** For interactive development on GPUs next to governed data, yes ([ML Operations use case](../use-cases/ml-operations.md)). If the team also needs a model registry, model serving, experiment reproducibility, or model risk governance, Domino covers that and Coder does not. Domino sells Governance as an [add-on](https://domino.ai/pricing).

## When Domino Data Lab Alone Is Enough

- **Teams whose output is models, AI apps, or agents.** Organizations whose interactive development is mostly notebooks, R, and Python for building models and AI applications, and that need governed deployment and approval in the same product.
- **Statistical computing in life sciences.** Clinical and statistical programming teams that need [GxP support](https://domino.ai/security) and licensed [SAS](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/work-with-pluggable-workspaces/sas-workspace) or [MATLAB](https://docs.domino.ai/6.3/platform-capabilities/core-concepts/compute-environments/use-external-images/matlab) workspaces. Domino markets a [statistical computing environment](https://domino.ai/solutions/life-sciences-sce) for SAS and R.
- **No mandate to standardize software engineering environments.** Organizations that don't need one environment layer across application developers, Windows workloads, and many IDEs.

## Pricing and Packaging

- **Domino direct.** Domino publishes no list prices on its site and [prices by quote](https://domino.ai/pricing). Tiers are Domino Cloud (single-tenant SaaS) and the self-managed Premium and Enterprise tiers, which differ in support level, admin and service-account counts, and customer success coverage. FinOps, Nexus, and Governance are add-ons. The pricing page lists Nexus data plane deployment as included in Domino Cloud, while the AWS listing offers Domino Cloud with Nexus as a separate platform fee option. We found no free tier or free trial. The public docs don't say which governance and audit features a subscription includes without the Governance add-on.
- **Domino on AWS Marketplace.** The [AWS Marketplace listing](https://aws.amazon.com/marketplace/pp/prodview-jiwfcjvc52zvi) offers 12-, 24-, and 36-month SaaS contracts. The listing separates a platform fee (with options for Domino Cloud, Domino Cloud for Life Sciences, Domino Cloud with Nexus, and self-managed Domino VPC Premium) from practitioner licenses sold in five-user packs, and Domino Cloud hosting and usage bill separately as consumption units. We don't know what customers actually pay, because Domino sells through quotes and private offers.
- **Domino on Azure Marketplace.** Domino has a [SaaS listing on Microsoft Marketplace](https://marketplace.microsoft.com/en-us/product/dominodatalab.dominodatalab_platform), and Microsoft's [catalog data](https://catalogapi.azure.com/offers/dominodatalab.dominodatalab_platform?api-version=2018-08-01-beta&market=US) lists five-user seat packs on one-, two-, and three-year terms. It is unclear whether a separate platform fee applies there, and we don't know actual customer pricing.
- **Coder.** Coder Community is free and open source. Premium is licensed per user annually and includes AI Governance, and AI Premium has custom pricing, with no list prices published. See [coder.com/pricing](https://coder.com/pricing) and [Packaging](../company/packaging.md). Compute is billed separately by the customer's cloud or infrastructure provider.
